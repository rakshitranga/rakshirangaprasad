# Project Oath v2: graded access

Status: **draft; not implemented or ready to deploy**. This document records
the October 2026 draft specification, the supplied database snapshot, proposed
decisions, and unresolved implementation questions. The current workspace is
not the Project Oath application.

Sources: the supplied *Project Oath: Graded Access & Tier Logic* PDF and
[project-oath-schema.md](./project-oath-schema.md). The latter includes the
user-supplied table definitions and RLS export; it is not a migration.

## Scope and vocabulary

- Tiers: onboarding, Bronze, Silver, and Gold. Platinum was removed.
- Account access states: active, action needed, and deactivated. A deactivated
  person cannot use tier benefits. Keep the earned tier as historical data,
  distinct from effective access, so that reactivation can restore it.
- Onboarding is a trial stage and cannot book sessions. Completed session
  counts are cumulative from joining unless a specific window is stated.
- The draft says that transitions should be logged with the user, old and new
  state, timestamp, and system or admin actor. Member-initiated transitions
  also need the initiating member recorded.

## Onboarding and tier rules

| Stage | Mentor recommendations | High-school profile exposure | Advancement or qualification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Onboarding | None; cannot book | None | Complete background, profile and interests, trainings, a pre-survey with LinkedIn URL, and three pre-med student referrals. |
| Bronze | One highest-weighted match | 1-2 students | Within two weeks, update profile and availability and complete one session with the assigned mentor and one as a mentor to a mentee. |
| Silver | 3-5 matches | 2-4 students | Reach Gold with five completed sessions as mentee, five as mentor, and fewer than two no-shows. |
| Gold | Five mentors plus one rotation, ten maximum, according to the tier table | 5-6 students | Highest tier; no further promotion requirement. |

On Bronze window expiry without qualification, return to onboarding. A window
needs a persisted start and deadline, and qualification must be evaluated
atomically with completion and the expiry job.

**User decision:** Gold requires 5/5 completed sessions and fewer than two
no-shows. The draft's 10/10 and 4.0 threshold is not a promotion requirement
because Platinum has been removed. The 3.0 Gold check-in remains applicable.

### Demotion policy proposed for v2

No automatic Silver or Gold demotion for a subsequent rating change. Session
counts are cumulative; no-show and inactivity consequences are handled by
account status, and a Gold rating below 3.0 creates a team check-in rather than
an automatic demotion. Bronze's explicit qualification deadline still returns
the member to onboarding. An administrative correction should be auditable.
This proposal is not implemented.

## Session, credit, rating, and no-show lifecycle

- Booking a 30-minute session costs one credit. Both participants must confirm
  the booking. Define an explicit pending state; the existing `sessions.status`
  check does not include one.
- **User decision:** a new member receives one initial credit by default.
  Grant it exactly once; align its grant and expiry with the confirmed credit
  lifecycle.
- On mutual confirmation of completion, update both roles' cumulative session
  counts, award the mentor a credit, update activity, and re-evaluate
  advancement exactly once. Store each participant's independent confirmation.
- Credits expire within two weeks. A transaction-safe ledger must record grant
  and expiry times and distinguish available, held, spent, returned, and expired
  credits. Specify what happens if a held credit expires before cancellation or
  completion. `profiles.credits` and `locked_credits` currently store
  aggregates, not issue dates.
- A mentee can rate the mentor after a completed session. Show the mentee
  rating after five mentoring sessions; a Gold mentor's average below 3.0
  flags a team check-in. Record which ratings count, prevent duplicates, and
  re-evaluate relevant requirements after rating changes.
- A first no-show sets action needed and blocks booking, rotation, and intros.
  Acknowledgment returns access to active while retaining the strike. A second
  no-show deactivates the member. Reports must be attributable to a session
  and processed idempotently; dispute and abuse controls require a decision.
- **User decision:** any no-show while Bronze deactivates the member.

## Inactivity, deactivation, and reactivation

- A daily job deactivates accounts after 30 days without qualifying activity.
  Define qualifying events, timezone, and the initial activity timestamp.
- Deactivation records the reason, blocks actions, cancels open booking
  requests, releases held credits according to the expiry rule, and releases
  the member's seat to the queue. These side effects and the audit must be
  transactionally consistent and repeat-safe.
- Reactivation requires admin approval after a member contacts the team.
  **User decision:** retain earned tier and unexpired credits, and reset
  no-show strikes after admin review. Expired credits remain expired.
- Re-entry when capacity is full, restoration of mentor assignments, and
  whether Bronze's qualification window pauses or restarts need decisions.

## Recommendations, capacity, and intake

- Rank mentor recommendations using the existing algorithm's availability,
  services, and specialty inputs; exclude mentors at their active mentee cap
  and those without sufficient available hours. Hour-based capacity should
  open seats as mentoring hours increase.
- The draft also describes Gold as seeing 3-5 matches and rotating once per
  week. Confirm initial count, rotation timezone and reset boundary, whether
  previously shown mentors can recur, and how to behave with too few eligible
  mentors. Enforce any rotation limit atomically.
- Intake is first-come-first-served by default, optionally weighted by
  completeness, with admin override for partner referrals. Record any override
  and its actor and reason. Define the completeness score, weighting, capacity
  unit, and seat-to-mentor assignment rules before implementation.
- Map applied and queued intake to onboarding and inactive intake to
  deactivated status; define how an admitted member moves to Bronze.

## Profile exposure, intros, and minor safety

- Provide the tier-limited profile exposure above and a viewer list showing
  each student's grade and interests. Enforce exposure limits when allocating
  viewers, not merely when rendering the list.
- Intros must be relayed through the platform with no personal email or phone
  number shared; include a link for session booking. Active status is required.
  Do not assume database storage alone prevents personal details in message
  content, booking links, email headers, or other existing profile endpoints.
- Under-18 students require guardian approval; the spec says an approval button
  already exists. Verify its actual implementation and consent semantics.
  Students need report and block controls; intros need a per-member weekly
  rate limit, reviewable logs, and message flagging. Define the limit and
  retention/access rules. These safeguards are required before launch.
- Enforce privacy server-side across existing profile and session access paths,
  not only in new screens. Database authorization, safe projections, delivery
  infrastructure, and UI controls are all needed; SQL alone cannot guarantee
  the behavior of an email relay or application interface.

## Existing integration considerations

The supplied schema contains `profiles`, `sessions`, `session_ratings`,
`profile_mentors`, `waitlist`, `notifications`, and `admins`. Existing credit
balances and locks may also support `submissions`; a migration must not break
that lifecycle. Check real grants, triggers, RPCs, scheduled jobs, and app
callers before changing these tables or revoking direct writes.

The supplied RLS export has multiple permissive policies. For example, a
mentee insert policy for `sessions` checks only the mentee ID, alongside an
assigned-mentor policy; permissive policies combine with OR. A scheduled
session SELECT policy also permits rows based on status. The effective access
depends on grants and any other safeguards. V2 gates and restricted profile
projections cannot rely on adding another permissive policy alone.

### Findings from the supplied functions and grants export

The October 30, 2026 export contains 23 public functions and 15 public-table
triggers. It is a point-in-time snapshot; it does not include edge-function
source, UI callers, scheduler configuration, or data.

- Session insertion locks a caller-supplied negative `credits_delta` against
  the mentee's aggregate balance. Cancellation or no-show releases the lock.
  A rating insertion updates a profile's rating and settles credits according
  to `compute_earned_credits`; settlement is not currently tied to mutual
  completion. These paths must be redesigned together to avoid double charging
  or granting credits twice.
- Credit deltas vary by student type and mentor booking cost. High-school
  members can receive a starting credit from an existing profile trigger.
  The new one-credit rule and welcome grant must not silently duplicate the
  existing trigger or break other student types.
- **User decision:** the new credit system must work for both existing and new
  users. Reconcile all student types and the existing submission credit system
  before rollout; prevent duplicate settlement on cross-tier sessions. The
  exact cutover treatment of already scheduled sessions and existing credit
  balances depends on the separately requested administrative reset policy.
- Submission reviews share the same aggregate credit and lock fields.
  Replacing the session credit accounting without migrating submission holds
  and settlement would corrupt balances.
- Session writes trigger a calendar-sync HTTP request. Preserve and test that
  integration; do not copy the exported trigger's endpoint or authorization
  material into documentation or source control.
- The export grants `anon` and `authenticated` table privileges including
  `TRUNCATE` on core tables, in addition to row-level policies and column
  grants. RLS does not govern `TRUNCATE`. Review effective access and remove
  unneeded privileges as part of a separately tested authorization migration;
  grants and role membership determine actual exploitability.
- Authenticated users have table and column update grants on sensitive profile
  and session fields, and the RLS snapshot allows users to update their own
  profiles and participants to update sessions. Effective safeguards must be
  checked end-to-end before treating credits, no-show counts, session status,
  or private fields as protected. New RLS policies alone cannot restrict
  individual columns where broader grants remain.
- Session ratings are currently inserted by a policy checking the rater ID.
  The settlement trigger does not itself verify both session participants or
  mutual completion. V2 must validate rating eligibility and idempotency before
  any rating-triggered financial side effect.

## Deployment and verification prerequisites

1. Obtain the actual Project Oath source, database functions, triggers, grants,
   jobs, and migration history; reconcile the snapshot against the database.
2. Resolve the remaining product decisions above and validate the existing
   member migration design below against live data and credit logic.
3. Implement a versioned migration, backfill, and application changes together;
   stage and test with representative existing data before production.
4. Test roles and RLS, direct-write bypasses, concurrent bookings and queue
   admission, credit expiry and cancellation, duplicate confirmations,
   no-show acknowledgment, promotion and Bronze expiry, deactivation side
   effects, reactivation, profile exposure, guardian approval, and relay safety.
5. Configure and verify the daily job and message delivery independently;
   define rollback and monitoring before rollout.

## Proposed migration for existing members

- Preserve existing account access and balances during an initial assessment;
  do not infer missing activity dates or credit issue dates as facts.
- Derive Gold eligibility from verified completed session history and no-show
  counts. Determine lower tiers from available qualification evidence, and
  queue ambiguous cases for admin review instead of resetting everyone to
  onboarding.
- Reconcile credit balances and outstanding session and submission holds
  against the existing functions and real data. Once the semantics are
  verified, grant a documented 14-day transition period to existing available
  credits whose original issuance dates cannot be recovered; do not duplicate
  held credits or give existing members a second welcome credit.
- Produce a dry-run reconciliation report and require explicit approval before
  any production backfill or enforcement. Exact SQL depends on existing
  triggers, functions, grants, application callers, and outstanding data.
